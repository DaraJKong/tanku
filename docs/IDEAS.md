# Tanku: ideas

Ideas that aren't in the specification. They are ranked by likely usefulness and none has been explored in depth.

| Idea | Rank | Notes | How to validate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pits | Medium | Holes that stop tanks but not bullets, as in Tanks!. Adds work to map generation and pathfinding. | Corridor prototype |
| Aiming laser | Medium | Tanks with Ballistics 2 or more show their line of fire, bounces included. Makes bank shots readable and fairer. | Playtest against a bank-shot enemy |
| Daily seed | Medium | A seed derived from the date. Worth more with a leaderboard on the official server. | After the modes exist |
| Pings | Medium | A key that marks a spot or an enemy for teammates. | Co-op playtest |
| Cause of each loss | Medium | The game over screen shows the tank that ended the run, but nothing tells a solo player what destroyed their tank while lives remain. A line during the respawn delay could name the build and say whether it was a bullet, a mine or a crate. The simulation already reports it. | Solo playtest: ask players what destroyed them after each loss |
| Arena ahead in the music | Medium | While the view runs ahead of a tank at the barrier of an arena, the music announces the fight before the crossing. The bridge has no mode to darken, so it needs a sign of its own, such as a soft snare with the second beat of its heart. It costs the surprise of the change at the crossing. | A switch in the playtest menu, once the music follows the game |
| The roll of an arena over the marks of its first wave | Medium | The fight of an arena starts for the music when its first tanks appear: the roll of two beats plays then, and the fight sections start a second later. The marks of those tanks last one second, which is two beats, as the roll does. If the fight started with the announcement of the first wave, the roll would play over the marks and the fight sections would start as the tanks appear. The same holds for each group of arena mode. The game on each machine is told of the announcement already. | A switch in the playtest menu, and an arena played with it and without |
| Whose weapon it was | Medium | Among several players, the game over screen says that a player's tank ended the run, not whose. The simulation would have to keep the seat of the tank behind each bullet, mine and crate, and the screen would name that player by their number. | Co-op playtest: listen for players asking who shot whom |
| Marks of a wave at the screen edge | Medium | A wave of an arena marks the places where its tanks will appear, and a mark can lie outside the view of a player who stays near the entry. A marker at the screen edge could point to each mark, as markers point to the last enemies of a sector. | Arena playtest: watch whether tanks seem to come from nowhere |
| Protection for a tank that appears | Medium | A tank of an arena can be destroyed the moment it appears, by a shot timed on its mark. It could be safe for a moment, as a repaired tank is for 1 s. The mark was shortened from 1.5 s to 1 s first, which leaves less time to aim at it. | Arena playtest: count the tanks destroyed within half a second of appearing |
| Updates through a store's own client | Medium | An early access release needs a way to update the game. A store that updates what it installed does that without code of ours: itch.io with its app, to which a build is pushed with its tool `butler`, or Steam. An updater of our own would have to download, check and replace the game on Linux and on Windows, and a page in a browser is always the latest. | Publish a build on itch.io as a draft, push a second one and see the app update it |
| A report from inside the game | Medium | An early access release needs a way to report bugs. A button of the menu writes a report: the version, the seed, the sector, the mode and the number of players, the system, and what the game logged in its last minute, with a picture of the screen. The player sends that file with a few words, by a form or an issue. The game would write its log to a file for it, and the build for Windows could then do without its console. | Write the file first, and ask a friend to send one after a run |
| Removing a player from the lobby | Medium | A run starts once every player is ready, so one player who doesn't answer holds the lobby for all, and a password only keeps a stranger out before they are in. The host could remove a player, who is told so. The network library has no call that ends the connection of one guest: the host would take their seat away, after which they are sent nothing of the game, and tell their game to leave. | Co-op playtest: count how often a lobby waits for one player |
| More tanks at the start of hard mode | Medium | Hard mode has as many tanks as normal mode: one in each of its first three sectors, two in the next four. With the balance of version 0.8.0, the regular player of the trials loses 0.02 tank for each of its first two sectors, 0.11 in sectors 3 to 5 and 0.37 in sectors 6 to 10, and the expert 0.02, 0.04 and 0.16. Sectors 21 to 30 of normal mode, which a player clears to unlock hard mode, cost the first 0.71 and the second 0.49. Hard mode costs as much from about its sector 11 on, where they lose 0.71 and 0.40. A player who has just unlocked it therefore starts with ten sectors that are easier than the ones they cleared. Over a whole run, the regular player clears 10.6 sectors of hard mode and the expert 14.9, against 16.5 and 22.6 of normal mode. The number of tanks could be the one of some sectors further, as experience and the cap on Muzzle are those of 20 sectors further. | Play the first ten sectors of hard mode in a playtest. If they are a warm-up, try the number of tanks of six sectors further, and play the runs of the trials in hard mode again |
| What is still open in arena mode | Medium | Arena mode is built, as an experiment. Three of its rules are settled: half of a group comes at first, or the whole of a group of two, and a tank for each one that is destroyed, the lull lasts 3 s, and the room is laid out anew between two groups. Three other ways were tried behind switches of a playtest and left the code: a group in two waves, a group all at once, and blocks and crates that stood again 10 s after they were destroyed. On the crude player of the trials, a room that is kept full lands about 30% more shots in a minute than the corridor, and a tenth to a sixth more than two waves did. Still open: whether a single player then needs a life more often than every fifth group, whether eight tanks at once are too many for four players in one screen, whether a tank that appears 4 m from a player after 1 s of mark is fair when it happens with every tank that is destroyed, and the word "group" where a player would say "wave". | Play one seed in arena mode from group 15 on, alone and then with the largest group of four players in a scripted run |
| Arena mode as the place to try impossible mode | Medium | In one room, what changes from one group to the next is the enemies alone. That gives the question of M7 a clearer answer than a corridor does: a group bred from the tanks that did best is fought where its parents were. | Build the breeding of M7 for arena mode first |
| Sounds for what the special bullets do | Medium | A bullet that splits sounds as one that bounces, and nothing sounds when a bullet that explodes marks its blast or when a bullet that seeks marks its way out. A mine warns with a sound of its own for 0.6 s. A cue for the split, and a short warning as the circle of a blast appears, would tell what happens out of view. They were left for the sound pass: a new cue has to be heard next to the music. | Playtest against a flanker and a grenadier that fire from out of view: ask what the players noticed first |
| A clock for a group whose last tank the players spare | Low | Players can spare the last harmless tank of a group to rest and to repair. In arena mode that earns nothing that the corridor doesn't give for free: the score counts groups, and a cleared sector leaves all the time there is. So no clock was built. If playtests show long waits, the next group could come a fixed time after the last tank of the one before it left the reserve. | Co-op playtest in arena mode: time the longest group |
| The next group at the players' call | Low | The lull lasts 3 s whatever the players do: it was 5 s, and was shortened because arena mode is meant to be fast before it is readable. A key could call the next group earlier still, and a longer lull could give more time to read a large one. | Playtest: ask whether the lull feels long early on and short later |
| A room larger than a screen | Low | The room of arena mode fits on one screen with its reserve, so that nothing fires from out of view. A larger one gives four players and their fifteen enemies more room, and brings shots from off screen back. The view already follows the tank where the room doesn't fit. | Widen `ROOM_WIDTH` and `ROOM_DEPTH` in `tanku_core/src/map.rs`, and play the largest group of four players |
| Walls inside the room | Low | The room of arena mode has cut corners and buttresses, and everything that stands in it can be destroyed. Pillars or short walls that nothing destroys would give ricochets more to do. The generator and the drawing take walls along the outline only. | Lay one out by hand in a test and play it |
| The tests of the network on a clock of their own | Medium | The tests of the network play real games in real time and compare a host and its guest tick by tick. On a machine that is busy with something else, such as another build, a dozen of them fail together, and whoever runs them can't tell that from a fault. A clock that the test moves, in place of the one of the machine, would make them give the same result however busy the machine is. It is a large change: the network library reads the real clock for its ticks and for what it waits for. | Keep the threads of the tests on two cores, where they fail every time now: `taskset -c 0-1 cargo test -p tanku_client -- --test-threads=24` |
| The tests on the numbers of the game | Medium | The tests are compiled with `tanku_core/test.balance`, which keeps the numbers of version 0.8.0, so a change of `game.balance` fails one test alone: the one that asks for the specification to follow. The price is that the tests check the rules on numbers that the game may no longer have, and a fault that only shows with the game's numbers passes them. `cargo test --workspace --features tanku_core/game-balance` runs them on the game's file. When the balance of version 0.8.0 became the game's, 49 failed that way, all of them for a number that they held, and sorting them found one fault all the same: the example tank that the help of the command line gives had become too dear for a player. The balance of the tests was then brought up to the game's, which changed 55 tests by hand. The tests that hold a number could read the balance in place of it: the whole suite could then run on both files, and the next balance would need no such pass. | After each balance that is made official, run the suite on the game's file and sort what fails: a test that holds a number, or a fault |
| A last ace that can be finished | Medium | From the 8 to 14 m that the reference players keep, the last ace of a group took the expert of the trials about a minute to destroy, where a veteran took 17 s, and one run in ten of that player stalled on it. What made an ace last from afar is that it saw a shot that is aimed ahead of it a second before it landed, from a gun of 9 m/s. The balance of version 0.8.0 has `foresight 0.7s`, with which it lasted half as long. The reference players now close in on the last two tanks of a group once none has fallen for 20 s. The duel then takes the expert 20 s and no run stalls, at nearly three times the tanks against a fast ace. So the trials no longer say whether the last ace is a chore: that depends on whether a person closes in. A rule for the last tank alone, such as a foresight that shrinks while it is alone, would leave the groups as they are. | Playtest from sector 22: time the last tank of each group, and ask whether hunting it is a fight or a chore |
| A price for the brain that follows its gun | Medium | Bred in the trials with the balance of version 0.8.0, the tank of 60 points that costs the regular player the most costs it nine times what a tank of the game costs with the same points, and eighteen times for the expert. Three breedings, against both of those players and drawn from two texts, end on the same make: an ace with an engine of 5 or 6 m/s, a gun of 13 to 15 m/s and a loader of 1.25 to 2.5 s, with a first shield or without, and little of the rest. It has no mine, no bank shot and no special bullet, one round or two, and seldom a bounce. The duels around that make say where it comes from: danger multiplies and prices add. Up to 15 m/s, each level of the gun multiplies what the make costs the player by 1.5 to 2, for 1 to 6 points. The ace tier takes it from 1.3 tanks in a fight to 3.4 for 6 points, and the same hardware without a brain costs 0.1. A share of the budget is the same for a tank that puts its points in its gun and for one that puts them in its mines. A tier could cost a share of what the gun and the engine of its tank cost, or the generator could hold the gun of a tank to what its brain is worth. | Change the price, breed again at 60 points, and see whether the best make still does several times what the tanks of the game do |
| What the breeding of impossible mode would end on | Medium | Impossible mode breeds each group from the tanks that did best against the players. In the trials, the same step ends on one make in about ten generations of 32 tanks, at 60 points and against a player who is always the same: the enemies of a run would all become that make, which is harder and no more varied. Three things could keep them apart: prices that leave several makes as dangerous as each other, a score that counts how unlike the others of its group a tank is, and players who differ, which people do more than reference players. | Before M7: breed with the prices of the day, and count the makes that are left among the best |
| A marker for a tank that fires from out of view | Medium | Against the regular player of the trials, four shots of the enemies in ten are fired from 20 m or more, which is out of view along a corridor, and more than half of them in the first five sectors. They seldom decide anything: 7% of the tanks that this player loses to bullets, 15% for a player who dodges nothing and 1% for the expert. What they cost is reading: the player doesn't see who shoots. A mark at the screen edge that flashes where such a shot came from would show it, and would change nothing of what the enemies can do. | Playtest: ask after each loss whether the player knew where the shot came from |
| What the sight of the enemies leaves open | Medium | Without a sight, an enemy fires at a player that it has a line of fire on, however far: four of its shots in ten were fired from 20 m or more. Since version 0.8.0, the balance gives it a sight of 30 m: it holds its fire at a player who is further, still drives toward them, and fires once they are within its sight, after the reaction of its sector. In the trials, that sight left every reference player as far as before, and one of 20 m took the player who dodges nothing a sector further and left the two others where they were. Still open: a player who learns where a tank without an engine stands can destroy it from out of its sight, unless such a tank answers a shot that comes at it or bullets get a reach as short as the sight. And nothing on a tank shows how far it sees. | Play the first five sectors and a late one, then the same with `enemy sight 20m`: do the fights read better, and can a sentry be destroyed from where it doesn't fire? |
| Every way out of a fight, in the editor | Medium | The play button of the music editor plays the thing of a tab alone, or the piece as in the game. To hear a roll or a connection, the fight is turned on or off by hand while the music plays, and the lead is wherever it happens to be. A choice that plays every way out of a fight in turn, as `connections.wav` of the listening tool does, and one that plays the tour of a run, would let a change of the connection be heard whole. | Change the connection in the editor and note what has to be done by hand |
| The markers of the last enemies as they are | Medium | A diamond in the red of danger at the screen edge points to each of the last two enemies of a sector that is out of view: a square of 16 pixels on its corner, which tells a direction and nothing else. It came with the first client and took the colors of the theme since: its rules were never tuned. Four things are open. Two diamonds can stand on each other and read as one enemy, when both tanks are past the same corner of the screen or close along one edge: the markers of teammates step aside from each other and from the diamonds, and the diamonds don't. A diamond takes its margin and its middle in the pixels of the window, where the marker of a teammate takes them in those of the interface, so it is off its place once the interface is scaled: by the arithmetic, 8 pixels toward the bottom and the right at 200%, which nobody has looked at. The specification names the markers of the teammates in the row of the heads-up display and not those of the enemies, and says nowhere what one looks like. And no test covers `mark_enemies` beyond the placing on the edge that both kinds of marker share. | A scripted run with the last two tanks past one corner of the screen, and a screenshot with the interface at 200% |
| As many crates as the progression says | Medium | The progression gives a sector 3 to 6 crates from sector 30 on, and the census of version 0.8.0 counts 2.4 on average in the corridors of sectors 31 to 50, about half of what is drawn. Two things take the others, counted over the 300 seeds of the census. Of the 4.5 crates that such a corridor draws on average, 3.7 find a place: the crates come after the blocks, each thing that is placed takes the cells within 4 m of it, and what is left to place once no cell is free is dropped. Then 2.4 stay: a sector takes away what it placed last until a way 3 m wide leads through it, and what it placed last are its crates. That rule takes something from four corridors in ten there, from one in five in sectors 21 to 30 (3.4 drawn, 2.9 placed, 2.3 left) and from one in fifteen in sectors 16 to 20 (2.2, 2.0 and 1.9). Either the generator makes room, by placing the crates first or by taking blocks before crates, or the specification says "up to". | Place the crates before the blocks in a test of the generator, and count again |
| What can still panic | Low | Clippy refuses the calls that stop the program when they fail, outside the tests: `unwrap`, `expect`, `panic!`, `unreachable!`, `todo!` and `unimplemented!`. Of the 53 that it found, 12 were written so that nothing stops, 10 were in tests, and the others are marked where they stand, each with its reason: the two readers that stop the build for a fault of `game.balance` or of `music.score`, three places of the map generator that hold by how a corridor is made, and the trials as a whole, which must stop when they can't go on. What those lints don't see is left. An index out of range: 540 lines index or slice without a check. Arithmetic that can overflow or divide by zero: 1,120 lines. Seven slices of a text, and the panics of the engine itself. Most of those lines can't fail, by how they are written, and each would need its reason said. The three places of the generator could also fall back on something instead of stopping, which keeps a run alive on a map that may be wrong. | When a player reports a panic: read which kind it was before a lint is turned on for a thousand lines |
| What the checks on GitHub leave out | Medium | GitHub runs the checks of every change on each push: all of them on Linux, the tests of the rules, the sound and the simulation and Clippy on the client on Windows, and Clippy on the client compiled for the browser. Four things are left. What a release ships is never built there: the programs for Linux and for Windows and the page, which a workflow could build and keep, for a tag or for every push to `main`. The page is compiled and never run: the checks in a browser that `tanku_client/dev/firefox.py` drives could run on a machine of GitHub, which has a Firefox. The tests of the client, with the network, run on Linux alone. And Windows builds there for `x86_64-pc-windows-msvc`, where the release is made from Linux for `x86_64-pc-windows-gnu`: `windows.sh` under wine still checks that one. | When a release is handed out: build it in the workflow rather than on one machine |
| What a release still carries of development | Low | The build of the client refuses a release that has its development features: Cargo can't tie a feature to a profile, so the build script of the client stops such a build and names the command that leaves them out. Two things are left. The server and the music editor have the same features by default and no such check. The server can't take the same one as it is: its benchmarks are built in a release profile with those features, by the command of the README, and a build script can't tell a benchmark from a release. That matters once a server is released on its own. And a release could leave out what serves development alone: the playtest of the command line, with `--seed` and `--tank`. The specification would then say that a playtest belongs to a development build, and whoever tests a release for the project would lose its cheats. | When a server is released on its own, or when a release goes to players who should not have the cheats |
| Shots that close in on a player | Medium | An enemy aims at its player, or ahead of them from the first tier of ballistics. A player who knows it gets out of the way by turning back or by stopping. Three ways of firing would answer that: a shot behind the player, where they are once they have turned back; a fan of shots across the places that they can reach; and a burst along one line, long enough to outlast the bubble of a shield. A fan and a burst take the rounds of a magazine, 0.25 s apart, and a turret that turns fast enough between two shots, so the model of a tank shows that it can. It could be a tier of ballistics, since it is about where a gun aims. Or a tier of brain above the ace, which mixes the three with the shots of today so that no fight teaches the one way out. Every bullet must stay one that can be dodged: a fan that covers all that a tank can reach before it lands leaves the shield alone, which the standard tank raises once in 9 s. | The duels of the trials: one tank with each way of firing against the three reference players, then a playtest against the one that costs them most |
| A barricade that only a blast opens | Medium | Nothing in a run asks a player for a mine: the way through a sector never needs one. Leaving out the rule that keeps a way 3 m wide would not change that. Counted over the 300 seeds of the census without that rule, no corridor is closed to a tank before sector 21, 2 of 2,585 are in sectors 21 to 30, and 20 of 5,180 in sectors 31 to 50: blocks keep 2.5 m from the walls and 4 m from the next group, so they narrow a way and almost never close it. What asks for a mine is a barricade that the generator builds on purpose in some sectors: a row of blocks from wall to wall. A mine opens it. So does a crate that stands at it, which a bullet sets off, and a tank without mines is then never stuck; without the crate, the garage must ask for a first level of Mines as it asks for an engine. It changes the first guarantee of the map, a way through without destroying anything. No bullet passes a barricade either, so the fight waits on both sides of it, and the brains must take it for a wall until it is open. | Lay one out by hand in a test of the corridor, with a crate and without, and play it: is opening it a moment of the run or a chore? |
| Messages that can't be missed | Medium | What a run tells its players is one line of small type at the top of the screen, where nobody looks during a fight: a sector cleared, a barrier unlocked, a wave, a life, an unlock, a tank destroyed. A larger message below the middle of the screen, near the tank that its player watches, would carry what matters most: an achievement, and above all a teammate's tank that is destroyed. Today that loss is the same small line, which names nobody ("Tank destroyed - a teammate can repair it"), with the sound of any tank that is destroyed. It could name the player by their number and have a cue of its own, unlike a blast, that is heard under a fight. Such a message must hide no bullet: few words, no panel behind them, gone in two seconds. A design for it and for eight other events, with their cues, is on the Design canvas "Tanku: messages that can't be missed" (2026-10-13). The user chose four of its options for a prototype: the alert under the tank (M1), the stamp above it (U1), and their two cues (D1 and S1). | Co-op playtest: after each loss of a teammate's tank, ask the others when they noticed |
| A shield that is ready again | Low | The shield of the player's own tank shows its state only in the table of weapons, in a corner of the screen. A short tick, and the dial under the tank lit for a moment, could tell when it is back. It comes back every 9 s or so in a long fight, so it must be quieter than any message. | Playtest: watch whether players look at the corner before they shield |
| A gun that is empty | Low | A click on the fire button with no round in the magazine makes no sound, and only the empty arc of the dial under the tank tells why nothing left the barrel. A dry click, quieter than a shot, would tell it to the ears. | Playtest: listen for players who say that a shot didn't fire |
| The messages of a run, again | Low | The menu of a run could list its last messages with their times, so that what was missed in a fight can be read once it is paused. Among several players, only the host's menu pauses the run. | Add it if playtesters ask what a message said |
| Chicanes | Low | Both walls take the shallowest slope together, so the corridor jumps 8 m or more sideways within a few meters. The throat between the two walls is less than half as wide as the corridor, so it only fits the widest stretches. | Corridor prototype: force one into a wide sector and drive through it |
| Camera that turns with the corridor | Low | The view rotates by itself to keep progress pointing one way. Saves key presses, but takes control away and can disorient. | Compare with manual rotation in the corridor prototype |
| Wider view at a barrier | Low | While the view runs ahead of a tank at a barrier, it could also show 45 m of ground instead of 30. That puts 66% of the next group on screen instead of 31%, and draws everything a third smaller. Tried in the prototype of the view past a barrier and not kept. | None planned |
| Shield state on the model | Low | The cue on a tank shows whether its shield is ready or recharging, for instance with lights that come back one by one. The white rim only shows the level. | Playtest: watch whether players can tell when an enemy's shield is back |
| What to expect from a tank | Low | A few sentences under the drawing of the tank that ended the run, on how it fights: what its brain does and how it aims. Each sentence must be checked against what the brain tiers really do, and reading them delays the retry. | Watch whether playtesters read the parts list first |
| Dark plate under the sight | Low | The lenses are a pale tint of the tank's hue, so they are faint on a weak tank, which is pale itself, once it has woken up. A small dark plate under them makes them clear on every tank, and makes the turret busier. | Playtest: watch whether players miss the sight of weak tanks |
| More playtest cheats | Low | A tank that nothing destroys, destroying the enemies of the sector, slow motion, a free camera, placing an enemy of a chosen genome. On the web, a playtest could start from the address of the page. | Add one when a playtest shows the need |
| Bullets that leave the barrel | Low | A guest shows a bullet where it is by now, so the bullet of another tank first appears up to 1.5 m in front of its barrel at a round trip of 120 ms. It could start at the barrel and catch up with its place over its first moments. | Playtest at 100 ms: ask whether shots seem to come from nowhere |
| The sounds of a bullet in the guest's time | Low | A guest shows a bullet where it is by now, but sounds its bounce and its end when the host tells of them, a round trip later. It works both out already, and could sound them itself, as it does for its own shots. A bounce that the host had otherwise would then sound for nothing. | Playtest at 100 ms: ask whether bounces sound late |
| A guest's own tank destroyed at once | Low | On a guest's screen, a bullet ends where it meets the player's own tank, and the tank is destroyed a round trip later, when the host says so. The guest could show it destroyed at once. A wrong call would show a wreck that isn't one, which is worse than a late one. | Playtest at 100 ms: ask whether deaths feel late |
| Bullets through the other tanks | Low | A guest shows the other tanks a moment in the past and the bullets in the present, so a bullet that destroys another tank flies on through it for a round trip before the host takes it away. Ending it where it meets a tank on the guest's screen would be a guess about a tank that has moved since. | Playtest at 100 ms: watch for bullets that pass through enemies |
| The sound of a repair | Low | A repair makes no sound until it is done. A tick for each second, or a tone that rises, would tell a player who looks elsewhere that the repair goes on, and the others that someone is at it. | Co-op playtest: watch whether players let go of the key too early |
| Distance on the markers of teammates | Low | The marker of a teammate who is out of view could say how far the tank is, in meters, as the ruler does for the barrier. It tells whether a wreck is worth the way back, and makes the marker wider and busier. | Co-op playtest: listen for players asking how far a wreck is |
| Fewer tellings of bullets | Low | A host tells where each bullet is at every tick, though a guest works that out itself between two bounces. Telling of a bullet when it is fired and when it bounces would take most of the traffic of a fight away: about 16 kB/s to each guest in a small one. A guest that works a bounce out wrong would then show the bullet in the wrong place until the next telling. The fuse of a mine, a gun that reloads and a shield that recharges are told at every tick in the same way, and a guest could count them down itself: four mines and two reloading guns took as much as everything else between two machines at rest, 6 kB/s. | Measure the traffic in a late sector first |
| What a guest keeps by tick when its clock is set anew | Low | When lightyear sets the clock of a guest anew, the guest moves the ticks of what its player asked, and of what it showed for that, by as much. That is right for orders that were reaching the host that many ticks late, as after a hitch of half a second, and wrong for the others: the host keeps an order under the tick that it first came with. After a clock set six ticks ahead by hand, a guest that had just turned showed its tank up to 0.7 m from its place for six ticks. After a clock set back, the host also ignores what is asked at ticks that it has orders for, so that a shot or a bubble of those ticks shows and goes away: when lightyear set a clock back because the host had stood still for half a second, a guest that drove back and forth showed its tank up to 1.5 m from its place for 0.4 s. And a bubble drops at a tick of the host, which should not move: when the clock is set within a quarter of a second of the drop, the guest sounds the drop twice. The guest could note each order under the tick at which the host takes it, hold its tank to the orders that the host has already, and leave the ticks of the host alone. | `cargo test -p tanku_client after_a_hitch -- --ignored --nocapture`, then the same with a host that stands still |
| The whole link in the lobby | Medium | The lobby shows and copies what the link for browsers ends with, because the game doesn't know where its page is published. Once the page has an address, the lobby can show the whole link, and copy that. | Decide where the page is published |
