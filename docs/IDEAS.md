# Tanku: ideas

Ideas that aren't in the specification. They are ranked by likely usefulness and none has been explored in depth.

| Idea | Rank | Notes | How to validate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pits | Medium | Holes that stop tanks but not bullets, as in Tanks!. Adds work to map generation and pathfinding. | Corridor prototype |
| Aiming laser | Medium | Tanks with Ballistics 2 or more show their line of fire, bounces included. Makes bank shots readable and fairer. | Playtest against a bank-shot enemy |
| Daily seed | Medium | A seed derived from the date. Worth more with a leaderboard on the official server. | After the modes exist |
| Pings | Medium | A key that marks a spot or an enemy for teammates. | Co-op playtest |
| Cause of each loss | Medium | The game over screen shows the tank that ended the run, but nothing tells a solo player what destroyed their tank while lives remain. A line during the respawn delay could name the build and say whether it was a bullet, a mine or a crate. The simulation already reports it. | Solo playtest: ask players what destroyed them after each loss |
| Arena ahead in the music | Medium | While the view runs ahead of a tank at the barrier of an arena, the music announces the fight before the crossing. The bridge has no mode to darken, so it needs a sign of its own, such as a soft snare with the second beat of its heart. It costs the surprise of the change at the crossing. | A switch in the playtest menu, once the music follows the game |
| Whose weapon it was | Medium | Among several players, the game over screen says that a player's tank ended the run, not whose. The simulation would have to keep the seat of the tank behind each bullet, mine and crate, and the screen would name that player by their number. | Co-op playtest: listen for players asking who shot whom |
| Marks of a wave at the screen edge | Medium | A wave of an arena marks the places where its tanks will appear, and a mark can lie outside the view of a player who stays near the entry. A marker at the screen edge could point to each mark, as markers point to the last enemies of a sector. | Arena playtest: watch whether tanks seem to come from nowhere |
| Protection for a tank that appears | Medium | A tank of an arena can be destroyed the moment it appears, by a shot timed on its mark. It could be safe for a moment, as a repaired tank is for 1 s. The mark was shortened from 1.5 s to 1 s first, which leaves less time to aim at it. | Arena playtest: count the tanks destroyed within half a second of appearing |
| Updates through a store's own client | Medium | An early access release needs a way to update the game. A store that updates what it installed does that without code of ours: itch.io with its app, to which a build is pushed with its tool `butler`, or Steam. An updater of our own would have to download, check and replace the game on Linux and on Windows, and a page in a browser is always the latest. | Publish a build on itch.io as a draft, push a second one and see the app update it |
| A report from inside the game | Medium | An early access release needs a way to report bugs. A button of the menu writes a report: the version, the seed, the sector, the mode and the number of players, the system, and what the game logged in its last minute, with a picture of the screen. The player sends that file with a few words, by a form or an issue. The game would write its log to a file for it, and the build for Windows could then do without its console. | Write the file first, and ask a friend to send one after a run |
| Removing a player from the lobby | Medium | A run starts once every player is ready, so one player who doesn't answer holds the lobby for all, and a password only keeps a stranger out before they are in. The host could remove a player, who is told so. The network library has no call that ends the connection of one guest: the host would take their seat away, after which they are sent nothing of the game, and tell their game to leave. | Co-op playtest: count how often a lobby waits for one player |
| More tanks at the start of hard mode | Medium | Hard mode has as many tanks as normal mode: one in each of its first two sectors, two in the next three. Against the crude player of the trials, its groups land about twice as many shots as those of normal mode in the same sector, and only land more than the groups of sectors 21 to 30 of normal mode from about sector 13. A player who has just unlocked hard mode by clearing sector 30 therefore starts with a dozen sectors that are easier than the ones they cleared. The number of tanks could be the one of some sectors further, as experience and the cap on Muzzle are those of 20 sectors further. | Play the first twelve sectors of hard mode in a playtest. If they are a warm-up, try the number of tanks of six sectors further, and run the trials of the modes again |
| What is still open in arena mode | Medium | Arena mode is built, as an experiment. Three of its rules are settled: half of a group comes at first and a tank for each one that is destroyed, the lull lasts 3 s, and the room is laid out anew between two groups. Three other ways were tried behind switches of a playtest and left the code: a group in two waves, a group all at once, and blocks and crates that stood again 10 s after they were destroyed. On the crude player of the trials, a room that is kept full lands about 30% more shots in a minute than the corridor, and a tenth to a sixth more than two waves did. Still open: whether a single player then needs a life more often than every fifth group, whether eight tanks at once are too many for four players in one screen, whether a tank that appears 4 m from a player after 1 s of mark is fair when it happens with every tank that is destroyed, and the word "group" where a player would say "wave". | Play one seed in arena mode from group 15 on, alone and then with the largest group of four players in a scripted run |
| Arena mode as the place to try impossible mode | Medium | In one room, what changes from one group to the next is the enemies alone. That gives the question of M7 a clearer answer than a corridor does: a group bred from the tanks that did best is fought where its parents were. | Build the breeding of M7 for arena mode first |
| A clock for a group whose last tank the players spare | Low | Players can spare the last harmless tank of a group to rest and to repair. In arena mode that earns nothing that the corridor doesn't give for free: the score counts groups, and a cleared sector leaves all the time there is. So no clock was built. If playtests show long waits, the next group could come a fixed time after the last tank of the one before it left the reserve. | Co-op playtest in arena mode: time the longest group |
| The next group at the players' call | Low | The lull lasts 3 s whatever the players do: it was 5 s, and was shortened because arena mode is meant to be fast before it is readable. A key could call the next group earlier still, and a longer lull could give more time to read a large one. | Playtest: ask whether the lull feels long early on and short later |
| A room larger than a screen | Low | The room of arena mode fits on one screen with its reserve, so that nothing fires from out of view. A larger one gives four players and their fifteen enemies more room, and brings shots from off screen back. The view already follows the tank where the room doesn't fit. | Widen `ROOM_WIDTH` and `ROOM_DEPTH` in `tanku_core/src/map.rs`, and play the largest group of four players |
| Walls inside the room | Low | The room of arena mode has cut corners and buttresses, and everything that stands in it can be destroyed. Pillars or short walls that nothing destroys would give ricochets more to do. The generator and the drawing take walls along the outline only. | Lay one out by hand in a test and play it |
| The tests of the network on a clock of their own | Medium | The tests of the network play real games in real time and compare a host and its guest tick by tick. On a machine that is busy with something else, such as another build, a dozen of them fail together, and whoever runs them can't tell that from a fault. A clock that the test moves, in place of the one of the machine, would make them give the same result however busy the machine is. It is a large change: the network library reads the real clock for its ticks and for what it waits for. | Keep the threads of the tests on two cores, where they fail every time now: `taskset -c 0-1 cargo test -p tanku_client -- --test-threads=24` |
| Every way out of a fight, in the editor | Medium | The play button of the music editor plays the thing of a tab alone, or the piece as in the game. To hear a roll or a connection, the fight is turned on or off by hand while the music plays, and the lead is wherever it happens to be. A choice that plays every way out of a fight in turn, as `connections.wav` of the listening tool does, and one that plays the tour of a run, would let a change of the connection be heard whole. | Change the connection in the editor and note what has to be done by hand |
| One type of bullet for each tank | Medium | Piercing becomes one type of bullet among five, and a tank fires one type alone: normal, piercing, exploding, splitting or seeking. The last three are the rows below. A gun then asks the players one question, and the muzzle says which. Still to settle: whether the type is one stat with a price for each choice, or a stat for each type of which a tank has one at most; and which classes fire the new types, since Piercing is kept for the lancer and the hue of a tank announces it. | The trials, once a second type exists: do two tanks of equal budget stay about equally dangerous? |
| Exploding bullets | Medium | A type of bullet that never bounces and ends in a blast like that of a mine, 3 m around what stops it. Its price is high. The bullet is white like every bullet and has a flat end. A red line cuts it near that end, a little wider than the bullet, with a gap on each side. It is the one bullet with a trail, a thin one. From 1.5 s before its blast, dashes draw its line of flight and the circle that the blast will reach. A time gives every gun the same warning, whatever its Muzzle: 13.5 m at 9 m/s and 28.5 m at 19 m/s, so most shots are marked from the barrel. On the tank, the muzzle flares into a bell, with a little red line against the narrow base of the bell, and has no ring. The little line has the shape of a ring of ricochet and differs by its color alone, which works because this gun never has rings. Blasts are neutral: a tank that fires at a wall within 3 m destroys itself, and an enemy's bullet destroys the enemies and the blocks around its impact. Brains have to know both. Still to settle: the price; what the bullet does when a bullet or a shield stops it; what is marked when a tank is in its line of flight, since a tank moves; and the circle, which is solid for a mine that warns and in dashes here. | A command of the development script that fires one in the game, drawn and marked but without its blast: the red line and the marks have to be judged in motion. Then the trials for its price, and a playtest against a tank that fires it |
| Splitting bullets | Medium | A type of bullet that becomes two normal bullets after a delay, one to each side of its line of flight. The bullet itself never bounces and has no stripe. The two that it becomes have the bounces of their tank's Ricochet, with a stripe for each. The bullet is white, with a dark seam along it. Two lines in dashes ahead of its nose show the two ways, 30° to each side as a start, and go with the bullet. Nothing counts the delay down: just before the split, the two lines are solid and flash. On the tank, a short cap with a slit sits on the muzzle, in front of the rings. Until the split, those rings alone say that the two bullets will bounce. Still to settle: the price; the delay; how long the lines flash, which is the only sign of when the bullet splits and has to leave the time to react; and the two bullets themselves, which are born touching, and which the rule on bullets that touch would destroy. | The same command for its look: lines that go with a bullet and a flash can't be judged in a still picture. Then a playtest against a tank that fires it, with and without Ricochet |
| Seeking bullets | Medium | A type of bullet that flies straight like any other and seeks at its bounces alone: a bounce sends it toward a tank instead of off at the mirror angle. It bounces as many times as its tank's Ricochet allows, so the type is worth nothing without that stat. It agrees with ricochet instead of competing with it, in what it does and in how it looks: a chevron takes the place of each stripe on the bullet and of each ring on the muzzle, there in the pale tint of the tank. From 1.5 s before a bounce, as before the blast of an exploding bullet, dashes draw its line to the wall and its way out, which follows the tank until the bounce. The bullet then leaves that way with one chevron fewer, and a bullet without a chevron is a normal bullet that ends on the next wall. It needs no Ballistics to find a bank shot, and it comes back off the wall behind a tank that dodged it. It also flies straight at its target, which makes it easy to shoot down. Still to settle: the price; which tank it is after, either the nearest that it sees from the wall, with a normal bounce when it sees none, or the target of its tank; whether it aims at where that tank is or leads it; which classes fire it, since Ricochet above level 1 is kept for two of them; and what a guest shows of a bounce that only its host can decide. | A temporary switch in the playtest menu that makes the bounces of one enemy's bullets seek, with plain lines for the marks: whether it is fun to dodge matters more than its look. Then the trials for its price |
| Chicanes | Low | Both walls take the shallowest slope together, so the corridor jumps 8 m or more sideways within a few meters. The throat between the two walls is less than half as wide as the corridor, so it only fits the widest stretches. | Corridor prototype: force one into a wide sector and drive through it |
| Camera that turns with the corridor | Low | The view rotates by itself to keep progress pointing one way. Saves key presses, but takes control away and can disorient. | Compare with manual rotation in the corridor prototype |
| Wider view at a barrier | Low | While the view runs ahead of a tank at a barrier, it could also show 45 m of ground instead of 30. That puts 66% of the next group on screen instead of 31%, and draws everything a third smaller. Tried in the prototype of the view past a barrier and not kept. | None planned |
| Shield state on the model | Low | The cue on a tank shows whether its shield is ready or recharging, for instance with lights that come back one by one. The white rim only shows the level. | Playtest: watch whether players can tell when an enemy's shield is back |
| What to expect from a tank | Low | A few sentences under the drawing of the tank that ended the run, on how it fights: what its brain does and how it aims. Each sentence must be checked against what the brain tiers really do, and reading them delays the retry. | Watch whether playtesters read the parts list first |
| Dark plate under the sight | Low | The lenses are a pale tint of the tank's hue, so they are faint on a weak tank, which is pale itself, once it has woken up. A small dark plate under them makes them clear on every tank, and makes the turret busier. | Playtest: watch whether players miss the sight of weak tanks |
| More playtest cheats | Low | A tank that nothing destroys, destroying the enemies of the sector, slow motion, a free camera, placing an enemy of a chosen genome. On the web, a playtest could start from the address of the page. | Add one when a playtest shows the need |
| Bullets that leave the barrel | Low | A guest shows a bullet where it is by now, so the bullet of another tank first appears up to 1.5 m in front of its barrel at a round trip of 120 ms. It could start at the barrel and catch up with its place over its first moments. | Playtest at 100 ms: ask whether shots seem to come from nowhere |
| The sounds of a bullet in the guest's time | Low | A guest shows a bullet where it is by now, but sounds its bounce and its end when the host tells of them, a round trip later. It works both out already, and could sound them itself, as it does for its own shots. A bounce that the host had otherwise would then sound for nothing. | Playtest at 100 ms: ask whether bounces sound late |
| A guest's own tank destroyed at once | Low | On a guest's screen, a bullet ends where it meets the player's own tank, and the tank is destroyed a round trip later, when the host says so. The guest could show it destroyed at once. A wrong call would show a wreck that isn't one, which is worse than a late one. | Playtest at 100 ms: ask whether deaths feel late |
| Bullets through the other tanks | Low | A guest shows the other tanks a moment in the past and the bullets in the present, so a bullet that destroys another tank flies on through it for a round trip before the host takes it away. Ending it where it meets a tank on the guest's screen would be a guess about a tank that has moved since. | Playtest at 100 ms: watch for bullets that pass through enemies |
| The sound of a repair | Low | A repair makes no sound until it is done. A tick for each second, or a tone that rises, would tell a player who looks elsewhere that the repair goes on, and the others that someone is at it. | Co-op playtest: watch whether players let go of the key too early |
| Distance on the markers of teammates | Low | The marker of a teammate who is out of view could say how far the tank is, in meters, as the ruler does for the barrier. It tells whether a wreck is worth the way back, and makes the marker wider and busier. | Co-op playtest: listen for players asking how far a wreck is |
| Fewer tellings of bullets | Low | A host tells where each bullet is at every tick, though a guest works that out itself between two bounces. Telling of a bullet when it is fired and when it bounces would take most of the traffic of a fight away: about 16 kB/s to each guest in a small one. A guest that works a bounce out wrong would then show the bullet in the wrong place until the next telling. The fuse of a mine, a gun that reloads and a shield that recharges are told at every tick in the same way, and a guest could count them down itself: four mines and two reloading guns took as much as everything else between two machines at rest, 6 kB/s. | Measure the traffic in a late sector first |
| What a guest keeps by tick when its clock is set anew | Low | When lightyear sets the clock of a guest anew, the guest moves the ticks of what its player asked, and of what it showed for that, by as much. That is right for orders that were reaching the host that many ticks late, as after a hitch of half a second, and wrong for the others: the host keeps an order under the tick that it first came with. After a clock set six ticks ahead by hand, a guest that had just turned showed its tank up to 0.7 m from its place for six ticks. After a clock set back, the host also ignores what is asked at ticks that it has orders for, so that a shot or a bubble of those ticks shows and goes away: when lightyear set a clock back because the host had stood still for half a second, a guest that drove back and forth showed its tank up to 1.5 m from its place for 0.4 s. And a bubble drops at a tick of the host, which should not move: when the clock is set within a quarter of a second of the drop, the guest sounds the drop twice. The guest could note each order under the tick at which the host takes it, hold its tank to the orders that the host has already, and leave the ticks of the host alone. | `cargo test -p tanku_client after_a_hitch -- --ignored --nocapture`, then the same with a host that stands still |
| The whole link in the lobby | Medium | The lobby shows and copies what the link for browsers ends with, because the game doesn't know where its page is published. Once the page has an address, the lobby can show the whole link, and copy that. | Decide where the page is published |
| A certificate kept between two launches | Low | What a host hands out for browsers changes each time the game is started again. Keeping the certificate and its key in the player's files for a week would keep the link good for a group that plays often and whose host has a fixed address. It puts a private key on the disk. | Co-op playtests with a browser: count how often the link is asked for again |
| A guest that tries again by itself | Low | A guest whose first try at the way browsers come fails at once is told that no game answers, and the player asks again. Under a heavy load, about one such try in a thousand failed in the tests: what listens for browsers on the host closed the connection as it opened, with an error of HTTP/3 (unexpected frame, or excessive load). The guest's game could try again for as long as its patience lasts. No player has met it. | Run one test of that way of joining in many processes at once, and count the tries that fail |
| A browser that comes back by itself | Low | A page whose tab is hidden stands still, and its host takes it for gone after 5 s. The page could join again by itself once it is shown, if the game is in its lobby by then. | Co-op playtest with a browser: watch for players who switch tabs |
| Pasting the address of a game | Low | The join screen takes an address a key at a time. Bevy 0.19 has a text field that pastes, which needs its focus and pointer plugins. Games on the local network won't need an address once they are announced. | Internet playtest: watch how players pass the address on |
| What a game is set to, in the list | Low | The join screen lists a game by its name, its address and its number of players. It could also show its difficulty and whether free mode is on, which a player now learns in the lobby. | Co-op playtest with two games on one network |
| The password in the link | Low | The link for browsers doesn't carry the password of its game, so a player in a browser types it. The link could carry it. That saves the typing, and whoever has the link then has the password. | Co-op playtest with a browser and a password |
| The password again in the lobby | Low | A host who forgets the password of an open game can read it nowhere: the game keeps a key made from it, and the lobby only says that the game is locked. The host's game could keep the password for as long as the game is open and show it on a button, as the host screen does while it is typed. | Co-op playtests: count the games that are closed and opened again for their password |
| A password that hides the game | Low | The password guards the door and no more: what a game sends is not secret, and whoever listens to the network can follow a run. The key that the password gives could be the one that the network library ciphers a connection with, so that only those who have the password read it. A guest without it would then get no answer at all, and the game would have to tell a wrong password from a host that isn't there. | None planned |
| Names in other alphabets | Low | The name and the password of a game take the letters that the lettering of the interface draws: the Latin alphabet with its accents, figures and signs. A player who writes in Cyrillic, in Greek or in Chinese can't name a game in their language. It takes a typeface that has those letters, which makes the game larger. | When a player asks for it |
| The tanks of the others in the lobby | Low | In free mode, a player sees what the others built only when the run starts. The lobby could draw each player's tank next to their seat, so that a team shares out its roles. | Co-op playtest in free mode: listen for players asking what the others built |
| Several tanks in the garage | Low | The garage keeps one tank. A player could keep several under names of their own, and pick one before a run. | Playtest in free mode: watch whether players build the same tanks again |
| A ready check after a run | Low | A retry or a new run from the game over screen starts at once for everyone, ready or not. It could wait for everyone, as the lobby does, at the price of the instant retry. | Co-op playtest: count the runs that start with a player away |
| All the best runs on one screen | Low | The main menu lists the best runs of one league at a time, with a switch for hard mode and one for free mode: sixteen lines don't fit under its buttons. A screen of its own could list them all. | When a player asks where a best run went |
| The best brain leads its group | Low | The heaviest share of a group could go to its best brain, or its leader could take the best brain that its sector allows: the tank with the most antennas would also be the richest. Both were measured when the points that tanks can't spend were settled. Nine leaders in ten are then aces from sector 16, or all of them, a quarter of the tanks of every sector change, and aces are already strong for their price. | The trials of the modes with it, then a playtest of sectors 16 to 25 |
| Waiting room | Low | Someone who comes while a run is in progress is turned away. They could wait in the lobby, or watch the run, until it ends. | Co-op playtests: count how often a friend arrives late |
| A port of the host's choice | Low | A hosted game always listens on UDP port 28405, and on 28406 for browsers, which another program on the host's machine may hold. A setting would let the host pick others. Guests can already type a port after the address, and the link for browsers carries its port. | Add it when someone needs it |
| Goodbye from a closed window | Low | A host who closes the window of the game leaves the others waiting 5 s before they are told that the connection was lost. Closing the game from its menu tells them at once. | None planned |
| A round kept for a bullet | Low | A veteran that can't get out of the way of a bullet shoots it down if it has a round, and it spends its rounds on the player as fast as it loads them. A tank without an engine therefore seldom has one when it counts: in the trials it lasts hardly longer than a soldier. It could keep its last round while a player has a line of fire on it. That costs it shots at the player, and all of them with a magazine of one round. | The trials: compare the two for the tank without an engine |
| Enemies that don't drive into fire | Low | An enemy that is far from its player drives straight at them, and a fast one is on the bullets before its brain has acted on them: in the trials, half of the fights of the fastest tank against bursts are over within 7 s, whatever its brain. Coming closer from the side, or stopping where it first has a line of fire, belongs to what an ace would do. | The trials: count the fights that are over within 5 s |
| More piercing levels | Low | A piercing bullet pierces one bullet. Each further level would pierce one more. | Playtest against enemies that pierce, once the price of piercing is settled |
| Hitbox as long as the bullet | Low | Bullets are drawn 0.9 m long but collide as a circle 0.45 m across, so the two ends of the drawing touch nothing. A capsule would match the drawing and make every collision test longer to write. | Playtest: watch for bullets that seem to pass through each other |
| Mixolydian music | Low | The piece in Mixolydian, the major scale with a minor seventh, with Aeolian kept for the arenas. Built next to Dorian to compare by ear, and not kept: the piece is in Dorian, which is minor. | None planned |
| Music that follows the place alone | Low | The place alone sets the arrangement, whether or not anyone fights: the verse and the chorus wherever the players are outside the bay, with the kick alone in a corridor, and with all three drums and the minor mode for as long as they are in an arena. Nothing calls for the bridge then, unless a timer does, which would put its break anywhere. Built as a switch in the playtest menu next to the music that follows the fight too, played, and not kept. | None planned |
| Electro-swing sound | Low | The same piece played by a small swing band, with every instrument still computed by the game: swing on every second sixteenth note, an upright piano that is a little out of tune in place of the pluck, a plucked bass, and a horn behind a mute for the lead. Built as an experiment, listened to, and not kept: it was not a good idea for this game. Its branch is deleted. | None planned |
| Key changes in the music | Low | The music moves to another key with the brain tier, or one step up after each arena. A change of key is heard when it happens and forgotten soon after, so it can't tell a tier. The cues, which play A and E, would have to follow the key. After each arena it would only freshen a long run. | Render a tour of twenty minutes with and without |
| Tempo that follows the threat | Low | Faster music for smarter enemies. Tempo is the strongest lever on tension, but 120 beats per minute is what puts the game's own timings on the beat. | None planned |
| Music in step between players | Low | Each player's music has its own clock, so two machines in one room play the same piece out of step. The start of the run could set the clock for everyone. | Co-op playtest in one room |
| Glow around bullets | Low | Bloom on bullets and lights. Bullets are bright and flat for now, which already reads well. Costs frame time on the web. | Compare screenshots with and without it |
| Smaller web build | Low | The web build is 38 MB, 9 MB compressed. `wasm-opt` would shrink it. | Measure the loading time first |
| Reflecting shield | Low | The shield sends bullets back. Fun, but hard to balance with friendly fire. | Variant of the shield prototype |
| Repair kits | Low | A limited, shared number of repairs. A tuning lever if co-op is too easy. | Co-op playtests |
| Something for a team that clears an arena | Low | An arena leaves a life for a single player and nothing for several, who have no lives. It could leave a team something of one use, such as a repair that takes no time. | Co-op playtests: ask whether an arena feels worth its fight |
| A life only for a fast arena | Low | An arena leaves its life only when it is cleared within a set time. It rewards the players who need the life least, and a clock in a fight pushes against reading the enemies. Set aside when the life became something to pick up. | None planned |
| A moment of grace for a tank's own bullet | Low | A player's bullet can't destroy the tank that fired it during its first half second, bounced or not. A bullet now ends on what blocks the barrel, but one that leaves the barrel and bounces straight back off a wall a step away still destroys its tank. Grace would forgive that too, and would let a player fire point-blank at a wall without thinking. | Playtest: count the tanks lost to their own bullet within a second of the shot |
| Friendly-fire option | Low | A lobby switch. Splits records and achievements in two. | Co-op playtests |
| Armor | Low | Tanks that survive several hits. Contradicts the one-hit pillar. | None planned |
| Stealth | Low | A stat that makes a tank fade out, as the white tank of Tanks! does. Contradicts readable danger. | None planned |
| Replays | Low | Recording inputs and replaying a run. Needs a simulation that is deterministic on every platform, not only generation. | None planned |
| Realistic military theme | Low | Camouflage, mud, weathered steel. Camouflage hides the hue and saturation that tell the players what an enemy is, and it needs textures the generated models don't have. | None planned |
| Synthwave theme | Low | Neon on black with a horizon grid. Everything glows, so bullets stop being the most visible objects, and it depends on bloom, which costs frame time on the web. | Compare screenshots with the chosen theme |
| Voxel or pixel-art theme | Low | Chunky retro look. The 1:2 slopes of the walls and the thin stat parts (antennas, muzzle rings) turn into staircases and noise. | None planned |
| Wireframe blueprint | Low | Tanks as colored lines only, on a black ground. Thin lines carry too little color to show hue and budget, and they shimmer when they move. | None planned |
| A size for each screen | Low | In a window smaller than 1600 by 900 pixels the whole interface is drawn smaller, by as much as the tallest menus need: the lobby of a host and a join screen that lists five games. The settings, the menu of a run and the heads-up display would fit a window 720 pixels high at their full size, with larger letters. Each screen could ask for the room it needs.
